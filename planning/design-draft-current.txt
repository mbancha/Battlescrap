Core Mechanics

Tactical starfall-fantasy skirmish. Bid for position, field three starter heroes plus three recruits, activate heroes through a shared action queue, fight with opposed D6 pools, collect Moonstone and complete tactical objectives.

Setup and Preparation

Deployment bidding: secret zone choice and nonnegative whole-PP bid; different zones ignore bids and randomly choose first side; same zone gives the higher bidder its chosen zone and first deployment/activation, with the winning bid added to the opponent’s budget. Tied bids receive secret positive increments until unequal. Bids may use any nonnegative whole amount.

Field the scenario’s three (or four) distinct available heroes and three free recruits. Buy equipment, extra recruits, group upgrades and extra starting draws before seeing cards. Only unused bid compensation survives as an attacker-reroll bank; ordinary leftovers expire. Roster access does not waive fielding cost.

Preparation budget: 15 PP for three heroes; 20 PP for four-hero team games (TEST). Starter prices follow the live sheet.

Hero element: randomly assign one unique active element to each selected hero for the game using its standee holder; assignments stay fixed for that game. Match its action-card holder and the element token paired with its tableau. The generic element cost means discard one tactic matching that hero’s assigned element. AP costs remain separate.

Fire (red flame), Water (deep-blue droplet), Earth (brown rock), and Wind (purple gust). Both factions use the same elements. Display element color/symbol together with faction color/insignia as a two-color identity code. For 1v1, choose one element to omit for both sides and use the same three active elements; four-hero games use all four. After roster lock, independently shuffle the active elements with state RNG and assign one to each hero on each side. Assignments stay fixed for that game. Match map piece, queue holder and hero card.

Deploy heroes and recruits individually in the chosen zone (north blue or south stone). Deploy and respawn on full hexes, including overflow placement. If full, choose a nearest reachable empty hex, choosing ties. Draw a starting tactic hand and one hidden tactical objective per side. Starting hand/deck quantities are TEST below.

Turns and Action Queue

Hero queue: six entries in 3v3; eight in 4v4. Queue entries belong to heroes. The lower bidder owns the top slot and alternating slots below; independently shuffle each faction’s portraits into its slots. If ignored bids are equal, second faction takes the top (TEST). Deployment/first-turn selection follows bidding separately.

At the start of a side’s turn, resolve its scenario scoring and check victory immediately. Then each living poisoned unit owned by that side takes 1 damage and discards 1 Poison, regardless of stack size; resolve defeat and victory before selecting a hero. TEST ordering: scenario scoring precedes poison. Select any friendly hero including slot 0, capture its AP/RAP entitlement, move its card to slot 0 and shift intervening cards.

AP can be spent on the selected hero or any friendly recruit. RAP (recruit action points) can be spent only on friendly recruits. AP and RAP may combine to pay one recruit action; spend RAP first by default, with the expenditure shown. Ordinary hero actions belong to the selected hero.

Hero basic actions: Move, Attack, Grab, Respawn. Each distinct action has its own use count during that activation: first use costs 1 AP, second 2 AP, third 3 AP, and so on. Move–Attack–Move costs 1 + 1 + 2 AP. Paid unique abilities have their own repeat counter; base 1 AP becomes 1/2/3, base 2 AP becomes 2/3/4. Card-only abilities remain card-only on repeat use. Printed “once” means once per own activation unless another timing is stated.

Each recruit has one shared action counter for the entire friendly turn, across all its actions and regardless of AP versus RAP. Move–Attack–Move by one recruit costs 1 + 2 + 3. Another recruit begins at 1. Counters reset at the next friendly turn, and persist through respawn.

Queue, bottom to top: 3v3 = 1/1, 2/1, 3/1, 3/2, 4/3, 5/2 AP/RAP. Team 4v4 TEST = 1/1, 2/1, 2/2, 3/1, 3/2, 4/2, 4/3, 5/2. Every slot is selectable. Entitlement consists of its printed AP/RAP.

End activation: collect automatic Moonstone pickups and tactic rewards and enforce hand limits; evaluate/claim at most one eligible objective and resolve 1 VP + 1 tactic, checking victory immediately; if the objective hand is empty, draw one if available; resolve the activated hero’s current spreadsheet end-activation ability, if any; discard unused AP/RAP and pass. Victory ends the sequence immediately. Regional effects follow the timing printed in the sheet.

Movement, Combat and Statuses

Move spends its current action cost to move up to printed Move. Occupancy, board bounds and impassable terrain apply; units cannot move through units. Attack spends its current action cost and resolves one attack. Grab collects a Moonstone on the unit’s own hex immediately; only heroes may collect. Automatic end-activation collection remains available to living friendly heroes standing on stones (TEST timing interpretation). Respawn spends its current action cost, returns one defeated model fully healed and status-free using deployment overflow rules, and preserves that model’s action counters.

Named abilities grant Push and Pull. For Push N, move the target up to N adjacent hex steps, each increasing hex distance from the source’s fixed position by exactly 1. For Pull N, each step reduces that distance by exactly 1. The effect’s controller chooses legal steps. Use empty, passable board hexes and stop when blocked or defeated. An ability specifies any additional effects. A blocked displacement does not cancel separately printed effects.

Attack and Defense are D6 pools. Roll simultaneously and compare highest face; if both are 6, compare counts of 6. Attacker wins every remaining tie. A successful attack deals base 1 damage PLUS 1 damage for EACH attacker six kept in its final result. A successful defense grants 1 Focus for EACH defender six kept in its final result. Apply damage bonuses only on a hit.

Defender and attacker alternate pre-roll tactic/pass; a play resets consecutive passes and two passes close that window. Use only the printed tactic effects or matching-element ability payments. Effects modify the relevant participating unit, including recruits; printed tactic effects work with any assigned element. Minimum final pool is 0: roll two dice and retain only the lower as the entire result; only that one kept die can count as a six. Declare damage bonuses before rolling. Cancel is available only during combat against the opponent’s immediately preceding tactic, including Cancel. Playing a boost instead gives up the earlier cancellation opportunity. Ability payments and out-of-combat tactics resolve directly.

Heavy Attack (x): a hit adds x damage. Attacker-only rerolls remain 1 PP per own die from banked bid compensation; automatically resolve if no reroll or card play is legal, otherwise explicitly finish rerolls before resolution. The counterattack tactic uses ordinary legal targeting/combat. TEST: a counterattack is one retaliation against the original attacker.

Five status types exist: Focus, Enraged, Fortified, Dazed and Poison. Focus adds +1 Attack die per token to the unit’s next attack roll; spend the entire participating stack after that roll, hit or miss. Enraged adds +1 damage per token to the next attack only on a hit and is spent after that attack even on a miss. Fortified adds +1 Defense die per token; Dazed subtracts 1 Defense die per token; spend both complete participating stacks after the next defense even if they cancel numerically. Dazed affects Defense.

Snapshot applicable status stacks at the roll boundary. Spend those participating stacks after resolution; newly gained after-effect tokens survive. Focus and Enraged are attack-only; Fortified and Dazed are defense-only. A successful defender’s new Focus can be used by a subsequent legal counterattack. A living hero gains 1 Enraged EACH time damage reduces it from above 1 HP to exactly 1 HP, including after healing within the same life. Statuses belong to units.

Poison (x) adds x Poison tokens on a successful hit. At the start of each owning player’s turn, each living on-board poisoned unit takes exactly 1 damage and discards exactly 1 Poison token, regardless of stack size. This applies equally to heroes and recruits. Ordinary defeat cleanup still applies.

Healing restores its normal amount of HP AND removes all Poison tokens from the target. A poisoned but unwounded unit is a legal heal target; heal living units up to printed HP.

All heroes have 3–6 HP. Hero HP and other hero data are maintained in the live Moonfall Tactics spreadsheet. Recruits have 1 HP, Move {hex:3}, Attack 2 and Defense 2 before upgrades. Damage reduces remaining HP; reaching 0 defeats the unit. Healing restores HP and also clears Poison.

Each HERO defeat drops one physical Moonstone at its last hex, draws one tactic for its owner and moves its queue card to slot 0. Recruit defeats resolve scenario defeat scoring. Secured stones remain secured. Resolve private overflow for the drawing owner even off-turn. Defeat, queue movement, rewards and immediate victory must each resolve once; Poison resolves at its own-turn checkpoint.

Three Fires Scenario and Terrain

Each scenario uses a map plus its own deployment/spawning edges, special rules, VP sources and victory threshold or explicit VP payout. A map can support multiple scenarios and multiple group spawning edges; scenario rules specify which are active. Scenario configuration defines victory.

Three Fires: water/north scores 1 VP at the start of each of its own turns, regardless of bonfire control. Stone/south scores 1 VP PER bonfire it controls at the start of each of its own turns. Control means more adjacent friendly units than enemy units; ties are uncontrolled. First to 15 VP wins; check immediately after every award. 1 VP per enemy defeat and no friendly-fire VP.

Later maps, particularly Timberwood, use structures. They block movement and are destructible by specifically authored abilities. Line of sight is clear.

Random setup: every map has nine X locations. For this map shuffle four traps and five Moonstone tokens randomly onto the Xs, all visible before play.

Any movement into a trap deals 1 damage, then removes it. Any movement into fire deals 1 damage; ending a character’s turn there also deals 1 damage. Resolve each entry separately, including voluntary and forced movement, stopping if defeated. Fire hexes are enterable. TEST recruit timing: fire end-turn damage applies to recruits that acted during that friendly turn. Moving an idle hero by an ability is not itself ending its activation.

TEST structure representation: impassable occupied hexes, with adjacency measured normally. Their attackability and cover follow explicitly authored scenario or ability rules. Preserve any explicitly authored edge-wall rules separately.

Battlefield modifiers

After the first scenario, roll a d6 once at scenario setup, after revealing traps and Moonstones. The result lasts for that scenario. Modifier rules appear in the scenario reference.

1 Plague: at faction turn end, each living unit adjacent to at least one poisoned living unit gains 1 Poison. Snapshot sources simultaneously. TEST timing.

2 Wind: roll one fixed axial direction at setup. Each friendly unit may take one optional free {hex:1} step that direction per own turn; normal occupancy and entry hazards apply.

3 Storm: ranged attacks have −1 Attack die.

4 Snow: ordinary Move allowance is reduced by {hex:1}, minimum zero; fixed ability/tactic movement is unchanged (TEST).

5 Heat: gaining Enraged from zero adds exactly 1 extra; add the extra once.

6 Moonfall: at setup add one Moonstone to a random legal full adjacent hex per trap; stones may stack.

Melee flanking is +1 Attack die PER other friendly unit adjacent to the target that has no adjacent enemy OTHER than that target. Recruits can assist; the bonus applies to melee attacks.

Ordinary ranged attacks use printed range and may reach twice that range at −2 Attack dice, following the live spreadsheet’s updated Range rule. Any ranged attack made while at least one enemy is adjacent to the attacker suffers −2 Attack dice, once regardless of adjacent-enemy count. The two penalties stack. Line of sight is clear. Non-attack abilities use their printed modifiers.

Battle Decks and Card Use

One shared tactic draw pile and one shared discard pile serve both factions; hands stay private. Each active element contributes 9 cards: two +2 Attack dice, two +2 hit damage, and one each Cancel, +2 Defense dice, counterattack-on-miss, Move {hex:4} OR heal 1, and Recruit assault. Total: 27 shared cards in 1v1, 36 with all four elements. Each player starts with 2 tactics, hand limit 8. Shuffle the shared discard into the shared draw pile only when needed; draw up to the available supply. Tactic faces show their element and effect. Center a large element picture in the top half; retain the existing effect layout and wording below. Keep internal stable IDs for distinct effects/copies; accessibility labels and prompts use element plus effect text.

tactic effects: During combat, Cancel the opponent’s immediately preceding tactic, including Cancel; cancellation prevents that tactic’s effect; before an attack add 2 Attack dice; before an attack add 2 Defense dice; before an attack add 2 damage if it hits; before an attack arrange for the defender to attack immediately if the original attack misses; during your turn move one of your units up to {hex:4} OR heal it 1; during your turn attack with each friendly Recruit. Matching-element ability payments remain legal.

Ability costs: one tactic matching the hero’s assigned element, plus any printed AP. Amplification discards are additional. Card-only abilities stay card-only on repeats; paid abilities use their own escalating AP counter.

Tactic draws: use the shared draw pile and shared discard. Starting hands, purchased draws, Moonstone pickups, objectives and hero defeats use the same supply. Enforce each drawing player’s private hand limit. Draw up to the available supply.

Moonstone and Tactical Objectives

objective deck: six distinct objectives only, one copy each. Keep one hidden starting objective per side, maximum hand 2, next-own-turn eligibility, at most one claim at friendly turn end, 1 VP + 1 tactic per claim, and draw one when the objective hand is empty and deck nonempty. The objective supply is finite.

Six objectives, each 1 VP + 1 tactic: v12-to01 Close the Net — attack an enemy adjacent to at least three of your allies; v12-to02 Break the Levy — attack two different enemy recruits in one turn; v12-to03 Into the Teeth — attack an enemy with a hero adjacent to at least two enemies; v12-to04 Pin Them Down — end your turn with three friendly recruits adjacent to the same enemy; v12-to05 At Their Gates — end your turn with at least two friendly units adjacent to the enemy spawn area; v12-to06 Claim the Starfall — end your turn with all of your selected heroes occupying printed X locations (Moonstone/Trap setup spaces).

“Attack” means a legally declared attack that reaches its dice roll; hitting or defeating is not required. Check adjacency immediately before that roll, after any pre-roll movement. Count distinct recruit IDs for Break the Levy within the same friendly turn; reset that progress each turn. TEST: all attack objectives count only on the owner’s turn while held and eligible. For Close the Net, the attacker counts among friendly units adjacent to the target. End-turn objectives check the board at the claim checkpoint; all selected heroes (three normally, four in team mode) must be alive/on-board for Claim the Starfall. Printed X locations persist after their token is removed. At Their Gates counts distinct friendly units outside but adjacent to a hex of the enemy’s current scenario-defined spawn area. These boundary interpretations are TEST.

Moonstone pickup by a hero removes the physical stone, records one secured stone and immediately draws one tactic and grants the collecting hero 1 Focus. Each secured stone gives 2 RP at game end, once, tentative for campaign balance. Repeated hero respawn/defeat creates new stones.

Preparation and Recruitment Economy — TEST

Current settlement pays 10 RP base, +1 RP for victory and +2 RP per secured Moonstone, once per battle. Campaign wins persist; first to 10 wins or a lead of 4 completes the campaign.

Keep PP, RP, VP, AP and RAP distinct. PP resets each battle; campaign RP and roster access persist. Each resource retains its own accounting. Whole player-facing prices; preserve half-point design estimates and existing half-unit currency storage where used.

Hero PP costs, stats, availability and wording belong in the live spreadsheet. Preserve stable IDs; TBD denotes an undecided field.

TEST purchases: extra recruit 3 PP; group Boots 2 (+{hex:1} Move), Shields 3 (+1 Defense), Weapons 3 (+1 Attack), each once. One equipment item per hero and one copy per army, kept on respawn; deselection refunds it. Trail Boots 1 PP (+{hex:1} Move, item maximum 7), Bellmetal Shield 2 (+1 Defense versus melee), Balanced Weapon 2 (+1 basic Attack die), Sighting Lens 2 (+{hex:1} ranged attack range. Offer purchases that provide their printed benefit.

Extra starting tactic 1 PP up to hand limit; reroll 1 PP per own die, attacker only, from banked compensation. Three free recruits. Purchased equipment stays with its hero and displays beneath that hero’s card. Purchase prices need retesting with the reduced starter costs.

World and Regions

One day, Surlune’s moon began to break apart, raining Moonstone across the world.

Not all at once, not all together. But they stormed through the land, some sprinkling as hot, white glowing pebbles that streamed down to the earth, others as massive streaking boulders crashing through the atmosphere and carving new mountain valleys or splintering across farms and towns.

But the stones, Moonstones as they came to be called, didn’t just destroy, they brought a will with them. People who witnessed that first crash saw terrible miracles. Where the stones had landed in a forest, some trees rose to the heavens while others started to move with purpose, smashing nearby lumberyards. Rivers flooded and swelled with life, deserts saw their sand turned to tiny gemstones, blinding in the light and home to crystal-scaled lizards the size of a horse. And the horses? And all the other creatures that found and got too close to, or simply lived near these Moonstones long enough? Like nothing you recognize as sane now.

People were warped too. Strange tales come from the furthest reaches of civilization still, but over time people learned to work with Moonstone. A farmer might find them bringing a more bountiful harvest, a carpenter’s poor cut might mend. Towns quickly became cities, and cities look to become kingdoms.

When a group of high-minded soldiers formed what they called a Concord (and they insist it had nothing to do with the wine-producing valley they came from) with neighboring cities to band together against the rampant theivery and other dangers on the road, it didn’t take long for that protection to be extended to neighboring areas that had not asked for it. Moonstone was power, and power was needed to fight back.

As the Concord grew, the many moon-worshiping Lunites saw a future in the nacent empire that they didn’t want for their homes or families. As word passed through the moonlit gatherings of the many villages dotting the land, talk of resisting the Concord grew. It’s not clear who started calling this resistance the “Wildmoon”, but the name stuck and only served to bind people to it’s purpose: an end to the Concord, a future where Moonstone is for everyone.

The players are Stonebinders, they have a special gift for drawing great power from Moonstone, healing grievous wounds and seeing their surroundings from the astral plane where they can suggest, or even command, those around them. Stonebinders make natural commanders, and this game is the story of some of them fighting for a better future on Surlune.

Home regions

Windlands — Tall-grass plains, proud clan leaders and prized lion-shifters. Brown, green and red grass-woven dress; Bantu-inspired fictional cultures. Ambush, speed and misdirection.

Highholm — Frozen peaks, frost giants, heavy furs and leather. Longhouses ring with feasts of mead and meat. Norse-inspired warriors turn stone-hard to endure.

Cinder’s Rim — A mysterious volcanic settlement of masked fire-priests and many fire gods. Carved tiki-inspired forms, ember rituals, magma and dangerous bursts of power.

Timberwood — Powerfully built, beardless woodland elves: axe-workers, growers and master joiners. Living public avenues meet towering timber halls. Hardwood arms and armor draw on medieval Japanese forms; every harvest is replanted.

Port Cutlass — Starfall whipped the island seas into a frenzy. A few sailors travel behind tamed whales or sharks; many now raid the rivers. Age-of-sail and Polynesian maritime influences meet wind-calling warriors.

These are fictional microcultures with the supplied broad visual influences; neither a region nor a real-world cultural influence determines allegiance. The Concord promises safety through shared institutions; Wildmoon promises freedom and self-rule. Both can recruit from all seven regions. Wellington — Moonstone-contaminated wells created dependence on its buffs. Phalexia — Moonstone fell during an army’s last stand, binding its survivors into mutually supporting formations.

Each player leads a stonebinder’s traveling company serving one of these movements. Both pursue the same deposits, routes and communities. Further showers of lunar fragments keep creating opportunities. A regional victory can secure access and earn local trust without ending the wider struggle.

Heroes fight for their homes, livelihoods and ambitions. Any regional hero may join either side: the same orchard keeper might seek Concord irrigation works in one campaign and resist its claims to the valley in another. Winning a battle gives first recruitment choice from that region’s shared pool; subsequent choices create different available rosters in different campaigns.

Ownership and pairing: show Concord’s maroon angular sun or Wildmoon’s sky-blue antler crescent separately from hero identity. Match each hero’s standee, queue holder and tableau element token by assigned element plus its silhouette. A small starter-faction reminder may differ from current ownership.

Shared Regional Roster

Starter rosters: Concord — Bisti, Hester, Voru, Tavi, Demea. Wildmoon — Niala, Perrin, Ketu, Ilex, Orro. Choose three from five in 1v1; use four per faction in 1v2 or 2v2, sharing faction resources (TEST). Region does not lock allegiance.

Every starter has one unique ability. Regional recruits have a unique ability and their regional ability.

Hero Ability Resolution

Read the live Moonfall Tactics spreadsheet for each hero’s current unique ability, cost, stats, region and availability. Document and code copies are implementation snapshots. Each starter has one unique ability; regional abilities follow the sheet’s availability field.

Generic payment: use the rainbow ? element icon on every tactic-paid ability. The hero’s holder determines the matching tactic for this game. The assignment supplies the element for that game.

Default timing is the active hero’s own activation outside combat. An ability-granted attack enters normal combat and uses the ability’s own counter and printed modifiers. The separate counter is a TEST rule.

Ongoing abilities: pay the matching tactic and any printed AP. The effect begins immediately and lasts until the character’s next activation starts or defeat, unless its text specifies this turn. Track each effect by its duration and active state.

Granted movement, forced Push/Pull and swaps preserve paid-action counters. Multi-target abilities select distinct units and resolve one at a time; optional movement may be skipped explicitly.

Hero-specific targeting, stacking and movement exceptions belong in the spreadsheet’s ability text and Notes. Read them together and ask the designer about contradictions.

“Within N” includes distance 0 for allies unless “another” is stated. Enemies are living on-board units. Healing can target wounded or poisoned units, restores normal HP and clears the full Poison stack. A combined heal/buff can target an unwounded unpoisoned ally only if its buff applies. Healing is capped at printed HP.

Keywords

Heavy Attack (x): on a hit, add x damage.

Light Attack (x): an attack can deal at most x damage.

Fly: voluntary movement may ignore intervening units and terrain effects, except its final space; finish on a legal empty full hex and apply its terrain.

Push (x)/Pull (x): on a hit, optionally Push/Pull the surviving target up to x legal spaces.

Poison (x): on a hit, apply x Poison.

Printed tactics and ability payments are exclusive uses of one discarded card. Cancel interrupts only the opponent’s immediately preceding combat tactic, including Cancel. Playing another boost gives up the opportunity to cancel the earlier tactic. Hero ability payments and out-of-combat tactics resolve directly. The counterattack is one legal retaliation against the original attacker on a miss, without AP, with ordinary range/responses; it cannot chain or multiply through repeated copies.

Presentation and component language

All game cards use their authored image faces in print and digital play. Players read those faces when choosing cards. Selection, payment and targeting controls sit around the card; changing wounds, statuses and current modifiers appear as clear live overlays. Accessible labels convey the same identity, costs and effects.

Hero cards are poker size, 2.5 × 3.5 inches. The name and class label sit above the portrait. Move uses a pale green numbered hex, Defense a blue shield, and HP one red heart per point. PP and region sit immediately above the attack band’s top line. The attack band is 22 points high, leaving the larger lower area for the unique ability. Ability names are bold, effects use first-person I/me/my, costs lead the text, and regional text appears where the hero has a regional ability.

Attack text uses a red sword for melee or green bow for ranged, then strength, range and keywords. Board distances use a cream, black-outlined flat-top hex around the numeral. Explicit range qualifiers stay readable. Dice, damage, HP, AP/RAP, prices and quantities use their own symbols or ordinary numerals.

Element symbols use triangular frames with pale matching interiors and transparent exterior: Fire red/pale pink, Water deep cobalt/powder blue, Earth brown/sand, Wind purple/lavender. Concord’s maroon angular sun and Wildmoon’s sky-blue antler crescent identify faction separately from assigned element. The same assets appear throughout cards, queue pieces, holders and the icon legend.

Tactics have a large central element and concise effect text. Objectives separate condition from the 1 VP + 1 tactic reward. Equipment uses PP and effect icons within its card text. Recruit cards have one heart, Move 3, Attack 2 and Defense 2 and follow the character-card visual vocabulary.

Character art

Use the current identity-correct masters in assets/heroes/current. Bisti Grasshade is h01; Kalawi Bloodmane is h15. Fifteen portraits are available; the live sheet identifies the ten playable starters.

Graphite and charcoal construction, broken fine ink, translucent watercolor and dry-brush gouache create tactile, visibly handmade characters. Use variable contours, economical hatching, irregular pigment and selective sharp detail on faces, hands and signature gear. Preserve each character’s species, face, proportions, pose and equipment. Readable silhouettes and angular forms support small printed pieces.

Earthy matte color varies naturally across cloth, fur, wood and battered metal. Near-white finely textured paper stays lighter than the figure. Faint class-color washes meet organically: primary class on the viewer’s left, secondary on the right. Striker dusty crimson rose #B85C65; Guard ochre #BE9650; Controller teal #4C9697; Support sage #829C72; Scout slate blue #739BC4.

Subtle regional traces suggest grass, mountain mist, ash, woodgrain, sea mist, well-water stone and weathered banners. Kalawi uses the provisional Striker/Scout art pairing while his sheet class fields are being completed. Asset crop and layout metadata remain separate from identity.

Equipment art follows the same paper, graphite and watercolor treatment: leather turnshoes, timber/hide shield with bellmetal boss, forged arming sword, and rock-crystal seeing-glass in carved wood and leather.

Print and play

The current kit is 16 US Letter pages, printed single-sided at Actual size. The first page is a concise bulleted rules reference with bold key terms and a pictorial legend of the icons used.

Order: rules; character cards with Recruits appended; tactics; objectives; equipment; two map halves; action track; action queue cards with VP track; standees; tokens; action-card holders.

Each map half extends to a 1 mm top and bottom margin with its aspect ratio preserved. The single landscape action-track sheet contains both complete queues in multiple rows. Each section measures exactly 3 × 1 inches, with AP/RAP, slot number and section dividers over restrained watercolor paper.

Queue portraits measure 1.25 × 1.75 inches. The 0–20 VP track is on their sheet in two rows with larger cells. Holders measure 2.25 × 2.75 inches, providing 0.5 inches on every side; blue/red faction borders are 1 cm thick, and element/faction symbols sit within the bottom 0.5 inches. Standees measure 1.25 × 5 inches unfolded and 1.25 × 2.5 inches folded.

Token sheet: ten each of Focus, Enraged, Fortified, Dazed and Poison; four traps; seventeen Moonstones. Components use the same current icon assets and paper style.

Digital presentation

Readable image cards wrap in the hand and objectives remain visible. Inspect by pointer, keyboard or touch. The current decision appears above the chronological gameplay log. Dice use an accessible click-to-roll control and a short animation.

An opponent’s publicly played card appears as its full card image in the middle of the screen for about one second. Sequential plays appear in order. The play remains inspectable in the gameplay history, and private hands remain visible only to their owner.
